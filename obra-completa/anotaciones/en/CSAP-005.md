## Historia

I wrote it around the third week of November 2016 in Vicálvaro and published it on 15 December. The title is *inmortales* ("immortals") with the e and the l swapped, one of the word games I liked as a child, like the last line, all run together: the letters are out of place because the immortals are no longer so perfect. It is for someone from the Scouts who seemed wise to me, who inspired knowledge, laughter and the urge to play. I idealised them a lot, and at some point I must have felt they were an ordinary person, who wasn't going to fulfil all my fantasies. The Scouts were then giving me a new space to socialise and feel more loved; idealising people was part of already living a fairly social life.

## Comentario de la forma

It tries to resemble six *redondillas* (a Spanish quatrain of eight-syllable lines rhyming abba), always with new rhymes, hardly repeating any. It is a stanza that lets you play: each new rhyme lets a new idea in with a narrative rhythm, and the eight-syllable line with enclosed rhyme gives the poem a lot of musicality. It doesn't fully mind the metre, as in «hoy, hoy unos cualquiera» ("today, today just anybodies"), where the comma works as a beat, or «Las cosas fueron a mejor» ("Things got better"), which slips in with nine syllables (of the eight the *redondilla* calls for).

It opens with a **paradox**: «Se mueren los inmortales» ("The immortals are dying"). And it sustains it with a series of words with a negating prefix, *inmortales, invencibles, infalibles, iniguales* (immortal, invincible, infallible, "unequalled"), which the poem disproves one by one, sometimes from the same root: «los infalibles fallaron» ("the infallible failed"). Other **antitheses** point at people: «los habladores callaron» ("the talkers fell silent"), «Los sinceros nos mintieron» ("The sincere lied to us"). The comma in «nos dominan, son normales» ("they rule us, they're normal") is a **juxtaposition**: it joins the two ideas without a connector, like a colon. There are weighty **reduplications**: «no es que murieran, murieran» ("it's not that they died, died", that is, really died) and «hoy, hoy unos cualquiera». And two **rhetorical questions** where the poem stops to think: «ahora, ¿quién cuida de mí?» ("now, who looks after me?") and «¿Dictaminar qué es correcto / o dejar que el tiempo pase?» ("To rule on what is right / or let time pass?"), which sets acting against letting be.

## Explicación

It is about reality and fiction, about idealising certain people and what happens when the myth breaks. That is why the title scrambles *inmortales*: they are no longer perfect, but they don't become "mortal" entirely either: they stay somewhere in between, *imnoratles*, because a trace of perfection stays with you. When the idols fall, the world seems like shit: «los vulgarismos de chulo / nos dominan, son normales» ("the show-offs' vulgarities / rule us, they're normal"), with a teenage, almost childlike outlook, the feeling that those around you are bad people and nobody puts them in their place. Without idols, the path makes less sense: «Continuar viviendo aquí / no tiene tanto sentido» ("Carrying on living here / doesn't make so much sense"), and the question "who looks after me?" sounds like the fall of the West, like [Nietzsche's death of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_is_dead), on a small scale.

Then it takes stock: «los infalibles fallaron / y estos ahora ya no existen» ("the infallible failed / and now they no longer exist"). If they fail, as infallible they cease to exist, all at once and in the present: now, already. «Lo lógico es ahora un chiste» ("What's logical is now a joke"): chaos. But "the talkers fell silent" may be a good thing, and that is where hope begins. The poem reconsiders: «no es que murieran, murieran / recordé que eran mortales» ("it's not that they really died / I remembered they were mortal"). Now from outside, it tells what happened: «antes eran iniguales / hoy, hoy unos cualquiera» ("before they were unequalled / today, today just anybodies").

From there it asks what to do when myths fall: rule on what is right, look for new myths that come from ourselves, become our own myths, or let time pass? And in the end it commits: «Los sinceros nos mintieron. / Las cosas fueron a mejor» ("The sincere lied to us. / Things got better"). Breaking idols, running the battery of fantasy down until it falls under its own weight, is good. It has to do with an idea of mine at heart: making the most of life, squeezing everything that excites you, playing a beautiful song to death until you stop liking it, and when one day you like it again, listening to it three more times.

## Anotaciones por verso

- "Se mueren los inmortales" [forma]: Paradox: what cannot die, dies.
- "nos dominan, son normales" [sentido]: The world without idols seems like shit: a child's view that nobody puts the show-offs in their place.
- "¿quién cuida de mí?" [sentido]: "Who looks after me?": the fall of idols, like a small death of God.
- "los habladores callaron" [sentido]: "The talkers fell silent": it may be good, hope begins here.
- "no es que murieran, murieran" [sentido]: The repetition weighs: they didn't really die.
- "¿Dictaminar qué es correcto" [sentido]: Look for new myths, be our own myths, or let time pass?
- "Las cosas fueron a mejor" [sentido]: "Things got better": here it commits, breaking idols is good.
- "Murieron" [sentido]: "Died": all run together, like the scrambled title: games from my childhood.
