## Historia

I wrote it around the second week of December 2016, shortly after «PoeMalillo», and published them together on 21 December. Jorge Manrique's *Coplas* marked me deeply, I used to listen to them with my father, and they look easy: short phrases, to the point, like little pills. And that encouraged me to write them. The title is the proof: it is "almost Manrique", an attempt at "Manriquing". I admit it isn't high quality, but my poetic impulses led me to write it.

## Comentario de la forma

It is a *copla de pie quebrado*, the stanza of [Coplas por la muerte de su padre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coplas_por_la_muerte_de_su_padre) (8a 8b 4c 8a 8b 4c), and here form goes with content: as in Manrique, it speaks of the passing of time and of death. The short line closes each half like a sentence passed: «y te mueres» ("and you die"), «si prefieres» ("if you prefer"), «te estás yendo» ("you're leaving"), «ya viniendo» ("already coming"). The metre fits without forcing anything and the rhyme is full from start to finish.

It opens with a **polysemy** of *pasar*: «La vida con todo pasa / mientras tú pasas de todo» ("Life passes over everything / while you don't care about anything"; *pasar de* means "not to care about"). Life sweeps over everything; you look away from everything. The same verb, two opposite dynamics, and in **chiasmus** too: «con todo pasa» against «pasas de todo», the order is reversed as the meaning is reversed. The short line finishes it: «y te mueres». The last two stanzas close with a **parallelism**: «te estás yendo» and «ya viniendo», two gerunds in the same slot, one leaving and one arriving. «Tus no infinitos instantes» ("Your not infinite instants") is a **litotes**: it could say "finite", but it names infinity in order to deny it, and so recalls it. The ending is an **allegory** of winter: «ponte los guantes / y afronta tiempos helados» ("put your gloves on / and face frozen times"), with the cold as hard times or as death.

## Explicación

The poem is addressed to a conformist person who doesn't care about anything, that is, who shies away from living actively, searching for truths, while time and life carry on their sweeping path over them and above them. The line «El que está feliz arrasa» ("Whoever is happy sweeps all before them") is almost propaganda for happiness, which has always excited me as an answer to existential questions: what we live for, what the purpose of life is.

Happy people are contagious and eat the world, however little they talk about it. So, if you aren't happy, rethink it: «cuida tu modo» ("mind your ways"). It is a critique of conformism.

Then comes the reminder of death: your instants aren't infinite, you're wasting them, you're leaving. And the ending is a warning, like the ["Winter is coming"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_Is_Coming) of Game of Thrones, which there warns that hard times are coming and you must be ready: put your gloves on, frozen times are coming. It may be death, or hard times, or the awareness of death, which is also a hard subject, something to face and to wrap up warm for. The poem doesn't close it.
