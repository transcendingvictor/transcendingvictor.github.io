## Historia

I wrote it around the first week of December 2016, in the third year of secondary school, and published it on 21 December together with «Casi Manrico»: I wrote them bit by bit and uploaded them all at once. I wasn't proud when I published it; I knew it was a simpler, sillier poem, and the title says so (*poemalillo*, "a bad little poem"). Jorge Manrique's *Coplas* marked me deeply, I used to listen to them with my father, and they look easy: short phrases, to the point, like little pills. And that encouraged me to write them.

## Comentario de la forma

It is a *copla de pie quebrado* ("broken-foot stanza"), the stanza of Jorge Manrique's [Coplas por la muerte de su padre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coplas_por_la_muerte_de_su_padre): two eight-syllable lines and one four-syllable line, twice (8a 8b 4c 8a 8b 4c). There it is used to speak of death from something very personal; here it is personal too, but it is used to answer back with rage, out of spite. The short line falls like a blow at the end of each half (hence "broken foot"): «y me insultan» ("and they insult me"), «no es mi culpa» ("it's not my fault"), «"anormales"» ("abnormal"), «son los males» ("are the evils"). The rhyme is almost always full, but it slips into assonance in the first stanza: *estereotipadas* with *nada* and *estereotipos* with *flipo* differ only by the s, and *insultan* with *culpa* rhymes only in the vowels.

It opens with a **paradox**: «Unas estereotipadas / no cumplen estereotipos» ("Some stereotyped girls / don't fit stereotypes"). If they are stereotyped, it must be because they fit stereotypes; well, these don't. The quotation marks around «"anormales"» are **irony**: they put the word in their mouths, as they use it to point at anything different as something strange, something to laugh at. And the ending makes a blunt **equation**: «estos cuentos de monas / son los males» ("these fairy tales / are the evils").

## Explicación

The poem is a reply. It seems they are attacking me («y me insultan»), but with the poem I attack them back: I defend myself. It is a speech act, the answer to something that happened. They talk a lot, they come across as chatterboxes («con cada palabra flipo», "every word they say blows my mind"), and in all that talking something may have come out against me.

Then comes the theme already present in the two previous poems: a group that judges others from closed roles, which you have to accept because "that's what teenagers are like". It is something very childish, very "you're judging me". The poem's answer is curt: «Deben ser eliminados» ("They must be eliminated"), and it puts those closed, judgemental roles on the level of the evils, of the problem. It doesn't develop the idea further: it is a poem with a lot of energy, passionate, written out of spite.
