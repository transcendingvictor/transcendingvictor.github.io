## Historia

Written around the last week of October 2016 and published on 26 November. It is my first published poem: before it there had been other writings and the odd little song, but this is the first one I put on the blog. I didn't have a fixed form yet: a capital letter at the start of every line, inherited from the poems I read in English class, and poetry understood as freedom, with sentences that come out of the soul but look for each other through rhythm.

At that time suicide was a subject that hit me very hard: how it is lived, how you see it coming, when people find out, how important it is to ask for help. I possibly had someone close to me who wanted to kill themselves and I didn't know how to help. They were people I could connect with enormously, and that was the hard part: that they didn't want to live. «Estefana» is a symbol of whoever inspired these ideas in me.

## Comentario de la forma

Free verse, in a single block, with assonant rhymes that come and go in no fixed order («algo» / «negarlo», «qué» / «bien»). The poem rests on **antithesis**: the sun and life against the rain, «Nosotras vivas, con luces» ("We, alive, with lights" — feminine in Spanish) against «A ti la ventana te va a matar» ("The window is going to kill you"), the "I" against the "you". That line is also the most hammered: the **alliteration** of /t/ («A **t**i la ven**t**ana **t**e va a ma**t**ar») sounds like four blows, like pointing a finger.

Length speaks too. The longest line, «Y mientras ves cómo la vela no se volverá a encender» ("And while you watch the candle that will never light again", 17 syllables), is hard to read, as waiting is hard; right after it comes the shortest: «Tú, te apagas.» ("You, you go out.", 4). Going out is easy. There are games of repetition: the **polyptoton** «Llorar ya no toca, tocó ayer» ("It's not time to cry any more, that was yesterday") and the **anadiplosis** «que yo te esperaré fuera / Fuera de esta vida ¿oyes?» ("for I'll wait for you outside / Outside this life, you hear?"), where the same word moves from a place to something far graver. And the ending shrinks: the **enjambments** «No nos vengas con problemas / de por medio» ("Don't come to us with problems / getting in the way") and «Si vas a explotar, explota / pero en silencio» ("If you're going to explode, explode / but in silence") drop short lines that sound like a door slammed shut.

## Explicación

The speaker is a friend, or a potential friend, of someone who is going to kill herself, and the lyrical "I" is feminine («Nosotras vivas, con luces»). She knows her and is fond of her: «Ya sabemos que te pasa algo» ("We already know something's wrong with you"), and she understands her signs, the rain, the window, the candle going out. But she doesn't know what to do. «Siento algo y lo ignoro» ("I feel something and I ignore it"): she feels empathy and ignores it; she doesn't accompany her, doesn't help her ask for help, doesn't pick up her signals. The one who fails is her, the voice. And a very adolescent outlook weighs on it: «Toda rareza cambia» ("Every oddity changes things"). What is different gets criticised, annoys, goes against the group and is rejected; anything that makes you feel weird changes for the worse the image others have of you. That is where «No nos vengas con problemas de por medio» comes from.

In the middle is the most confusing part: «La marea bajará / Mientras tanto no te ahogues / Que yo te esperaré fuera / Fuera de esta vida ¿oyes?» ("The tide will go out / Meanwhile don't drown / For I'll wait for you outside / Outside this life, you hear?"). The sea recalls death, and there is also a "hold on, better times will come". "I'll wait for you outside" has two faces. On one side it is keeping her company: I'm here too, I'm waiting for you, and I promise you an "outside this life", a friendship for this life and the next, or the fantasy that even if you die I'll come back to you. On the other, it is staying outside: I watch you from the shore, I don't want to get wet, I don't want to go down into your sea, your anxieties and your drownings. There is a will to accompany her, but also a foot held back: I don't know what to do, I don't want any trouble, I don't want to be the bad one. It is a divided heart, one that doesn't judge but that, to some extent, accepts that her friend may kill herself.

The ending can be read as part of that grief, even as hatred towards that person: it's over, she was such a pain, let's move on. «Nuestras vidas continúan» ("Our lives go on"). That is why the voice is unclear: it can be the friend who fails, someone who treats her badly and pushes her, or a suicidal voice in her head promising her peace. And the "you" is her, that person who wants to kill herself, but it is also you, the reader. Because, although the subject is suicide, the poem speaks of something more general: everything that makes you feel like a freak, the mental loops you shut yourself into, keeps pulling you away from people and takes away your wish to ask for help when you need it, and that makes you feel worse. It is exclusion and isolation: the "don't bother anyone, don't ask for help, just die on your own", one person's intimacy against everyone else.

## Anotaciones por verso

- "Nosotras" [sentido]: Feminine. For a large part of my life, naming and recognising myself in the feminine has been a plausible option I identified with; it will appear in more poems.
- "Toda rareza cambia" [sentido]: "Every oddity changes things": anything that makes you feel weird changes for the worse what the group sees in you. It announces the "don't come to us with problems".
- "A ti la ventana te va a matar" [forma]: Four /t/ sounds: four blows, like pointing a finger.
- "Tú, te apagas." [forma]: The shortest line, right after the longest: waiting is hard, going out is easy.
- "fuera" [sentido]: "Outside": I'll wait for you outside, I'll keep you company, but from the shore, without getting wet.
- "Siento algo y lo ignoro" [sentido]: She feels empathy and ignores it. The one who fails is the voice.
- "No nos vengas con problemas" [sentido]: The trouble with being odd; or grief: that's it, let's move on.
- "(estefana)": A symbol of whoever inspired these ideas in me.

## Forma

- v1 [forma]: 10 syllables, with a hiatus in «pasa | algo»: it sounds better, and free verse allows it.
- v10 [forma]: «real» as one syllable, as it is said in practice.
- v11 [forma]: The longest line of the poem, right before the shortest.
- v16 [forma]: No elision before «¿oyes?»: the question stands apart. With the three lines before it, a touch of *romance* (Spanish ballad): octosyllables rhyming on the even lines.
- v18 [forma]: Elision in «miro y».
- v19 [forma]: No elision in «Siento | algo», to match the rhythm of the previous line; elision in «algo y».
- v20 [forma]: Colon after «cede».
- v22 [forma]: Internal rhyme: «vengas» and «problemas» rhyme within the line.
- v24 [forma]: No elision in «calla | aunque»: there is a short pause.
